To the Reader

You hold a primer prepared by the University of the Five in Zenith for those who come new to the Realm: travellers from the far provinces, the newly sworn, the young, and any who were never taught. It is written under the First Edict, which makes knowledge free to all, and it is set down in the seven hundred and forty-third year since Landfall.

It tells you what any well-taught person of the Concord knows: where we came from, what we swore, how we live, who shares this world with us, and where the roads end. It does not tell you everything. No honest book does.

Part I

The New Era

1. The World in One Page

Before the world shattered, we walked blind. Now we walk the Path: not to escape the ruin, but to rise from it.The Scroll of Becoming

Aurelis is a civilization founded by the survivors of another world.

Eight centuries ago, the peoples of Cael'Vatra escaped a dying realm aboard Velzaar's Ark. They crossed the void in a century of enchanted sleep and awakened beneath an unfamiliar sky. Their old nations were gone. Their gods had failed them. Their histories were full of conquest, betrayal, and extinction.

The survivors chose to begin again.

On the northern shore of Goldwater Bay they founded Zenith, a capital governed not by a monarch but by a council whose delegates swear a binding oath to speak truth, declare their interests, obey lawful judgement, and surrender power when their term ends. The philosophy called the Path became the moral foundation of the new age. Guest Right was made universal. Healing and education became public duties. A common coin eased trade among peoples who had once been enemies. On the tower-island at the heart of the bay, Bram the Founder raised Avalon and gave it to the Custodes, an order sworn to no crown, so that no local war could ever again become a global one.

Before he laid down his power, Bram bound three of these promises into the fabric of the world itself. These are the Three Edicts, and no one has broken them without paying.

For seven centuries, it has held.

Aurelis today is a realm of steam railways, rune-driven engines, airships, public academies, houses of mending, ancient forests, giant citadels, a city built among the roots of the greatest tree in the world, and island peoples adapted to the sea. Its weapons and armour still resemble those of an older age, because destructive invention is forbidden and the mass production of killing tools is considered a betrayal of the Founding.

The peace has lasted so long that most people no longer remember what it cost. And peace among peoples is not the same as a safe world. Beyond the rail lines and the wayhouses lie territorial beasts, elementals, islands that move, caverns no one has mapped, and leviathans the size of cities.

This is the world you have inherited. The rest of this primer is the account of how it came to be, and how it is kept.

2. What Every Child Is Taught

Only when the mind is still, may the self be heard.On Silence and Listening, the first chapter of the Scroll

Children throughout the Concord are taught five foundational truths before they are taught anything else:

  1. The old world died because power grew faster than wisdom.
  2. No people survived alone. Humans, Elevaari, Durnin, Golgoth, the peoples of Sporefall, Proteans, and others reached Aurelis because enemies chose cooperation over extinction.
  3. The Path is not a promise of perfection. It is a discipline of becoming better than one was yesterday.
  4. An oath has weight. Words can create power, and breaking sacred words can break the person who spoke them.
  5. Peace is work. It is not the natural state of the world, and it does not maintain itself.

Children also learn the names that bind the present to the Founding: the relics Twilight, the Finished Chronicle, and Godfalla; the institutions of the Five, the Haven of the Lost Stars, the Council of Many Voices, and the Custodes; and the Companions of the Dawn, remembered by the titles the stories gave them: Bram the Founder, Heckleberry the Unlikely Hero, Milo the Trickster, Clay the Giantborn, Theodore the Unlucky, Kaelen the Martyr, and the one whose name is spoken as a warning, Oberon the Betrayer.

They grow up knowing that danger has not disappeared. Settlements drill for emergencies, the Custodes answer sightings, and even a prosperous city keeps shelters for the day a leviathan comes. Courage is expected of a knight or an explorer, but it is also expected of the teacher, the healer, the farmer, and the neighbour who helps others reach safety.

3. The Story So Far

Is this our destiny? No, merely our nature.Solmir and Velzaar, in dialogue, as the Chronicle records it

Every people of the Concord tells this story, and no two tell it alike. What follows is the account the Five teaches, drawn from the Finished Chronicle, which was carried through most of these events and does not lie. It is told in the order the Chronicle gives it.

The God-Killers

Humanity did not begin on the old world. It came from the stars, and where it settled, destruction followed in an endless cycle of betrayal and destruction, of a history erased and forgotten and new beginnings. Once more they ventured in the stars and once more they found a home. The native peoples of this last home, the Elevaari among them, welcomed the newcomers and then turned against them, when human science and magic grew faster than any neighbour could bear. The last cycle ended with the impossible. Humanity killed a god: Hythrea, god of the Elevaari.

The other gods did not avenge him. They abandoned humanity and the world itself, afraid of sharing his fate, and left the mortals facing a dying planet. Hythrea's essence could not comprehend its own death. It lingered, and it spoiled. It became the Blight, a corrupting plague that raised the dead and consumed the living, and it spread.

Cael'Vatra and the Crucible

Five mortals had stood together against Hythrea: Velzaar, Solmir, Nyssara, Elaria and Korraneth. With the world at the edge of ruin they gave humanity one last chance: change, or die. They raised the haven of Cael'Vatra, warded against the Blight, and there humanity lived for a thousand years while the world outside rotted.

In Cael'Vatra humanity was guided by the Path and grew spiritually instead of technologically. Its people became attuned to the four Laws of the universe, Divine, Primal, Elemental and Arcane, and that attunement changed their bodies and gave them new powers. Every citizen passed through a Crucible on coming of age to awaken that change. The Five became, to those they sheltered, gods.

The Fall of Cael'Vatra

The Blight comes for all. When it came for Cael'Vatra, the haven fell. Its people lost their world and watched their loved ones die and rise again, and the survivors fled underground into an artificial realm beneath a false sky, determined to learn what their world truly was.

They learned pieces of their history, and in time they came face to face with some of the Five. A thousand years of solitary service had broken those minds. The shepherds and protectors had become destroyers.

The goddess Nyssara, working from the shadows, steered a band of adventurers through those events: Clay Rostov, Theodore Firewarden, Kaelen Thorne, Milo Vexler and Bram Alderwind. She led them to her sister Elaria, who had spent the age protecting what life remained from a throne built to drain her and feed the land. Nyssara ended her sister's life, and the act woke their father, the god Korraneth.

The Tower and the Devil

While the band searched the hidden realm, they found the Blight already devouring it. They took refuge in a nearby tower, which proved to belong to the Simulacrum of a wizard named Irenicus, once a student of Velzaar. There they also met a stranger creature, a Devil, who offered power and knowledge in exchange for a favour. Clay, Kaelen and Theodore accepted and signed its infernal contracts. The bargain bought them the fight against the Simulacrum, but not the whole of it: Theodore was destroyed in the final battle by a stray spell, in the wrong place at the wrong moment.

Korraneth, broken and grieving, went to Revathra, the last bastion of humanity, took back Elaria's body and destroyed the city. The ground shattered under his blows and the band fell into a vast cavern beneath the world. Nyssara intervened and saved them. In the dark they found one more survivor, Oberon, a knight of the Avowed, who joined them. Alone, perhaps the last of their kind, they resolved to uncover the mystery of their world and, at the least, to take vengeance for it.

Sporefall

In the caverns they found Sporefall, a city of survivors of the first cataclysm, the one Hythrea's death had caused. It was ruled by Arbiter Ilyssara Zaelyth, a Nhalari of the old world, and by Warden Threxil Zai'Ryn, a Zerth stranded when the gods left and travel between realms became impossible. The city owed its way of life to the Myconid Sovereign Kel'Thuun, whose spores crossed every barrier of language and whose dead fed the fields. Under the Warden and the Arbiter the rule was harsh and fair: everyone received what they needed to live, so long as they gave back to the community, kept from violence and traded honestly.

Nyssara joined them there and named their destination: Aurith Solmir, where the god Solmir rested and waited for the end. A barrier protected the citadel, and only a weapon of sufficient power could breach it. That weapon would be Bram's dagger, which now held Elaria's essence. To complete it, Korraneth, whom Nyssara had put to sleep for one month, would have to be slain with the dagger and his essence drawn into it, and that could only be done with the Warden's help, for his Zerth blade could sever a god, for a time, from the divinity that made him invulnerable.

Starmetal and Emberhold

The band chose to earn the Warden's trust first, and he set them to the needs of Sporefall's people. One errand for a merchant led to an ancient mine of Starmetal, a sacred ore that could be forged into weapons able to wound a god. There Milo was nearly swallowed by a gigantic worm, and Kaelen broke his contract with the devil for the first time to save him. The cost was dire; Kaelen chose to bear it. Deeper in, the band learned that Starmetal was the flesh of dead celestials, and that one still lived, driven mad by the god that had abandoned it. Oberon would not harm it, and while he held back, it singled out those who had taken the devil's bargain and nearly killed them. The others escaped by teleportation magic and Oberon chose to stay behind. He gave the fallen angel the one comfort left to it, death in honourable combat, and was rewarded with its holy blade. When the band reunited in Sporefall, trust in him was gone. They gave him another chance, but watched his preaching about a voice he called the Light with growing unease.

Their next road led to Emberhold, the last bastion of the Volmyr, the fire giants, where their equipment might be made ready for Korraneth. Only one Volmyr remained: Thormund the Last, brother of a king who had died sealing the ancient forge together with a deadly elemental the giants had unearthed by digging too close to the surface. The Volmyr were prone to Slokna, which in their tongue means to be extinguished: left without purpose, a fire giant falls into despair, its flame dies, and so does it. The band destroyed the elemental and unsealed the forge, and Thormund made for them equipment of the highest quality, among it the sword Twilight, a blade made from Starmetal and the hilt of a greatsword belonging to the warrior called Virelle Daos, who had fallen in the first battle against Korraneth, and which now contained a part of her soul.

The Lich and the Chronicler

To unlock the power sleeping in them, the band sought the dragon Aurelith. Her last egg had been stolen by undead raiders, and she would help them only when it was returned. They followed the trail into a dungeon that proved to be the lair of Irenicus, now a lich, and defeated him. In that fight Kaelen broke his contract for a second time, and the devil killed him where he stood. His last words to his companions were "Make it count."

It was after Kaelen's death that Heckleberry Finn joined the band: a historian of Sporefall who carried his family's heirloom, a magical shield called the Unfinished Chronicle, which had recorded the band's previous exploits, and set himself to recording everything that followed. The Finished Chronicle that the Five keeps in Zenith is that shield.

The Ancient One

Through all of this Oberon spoke more and more of the Light that guided him and served as his conscience, and it drove him to extremes that set him against his companions. He began to see Solmir in visions, asking for his help and blessing his sword. When the band vanquished an ancient foe of the Warden and so secured his help, the Warden told them that early human expeditions into the underground, hundreds of years before, might yet live, enslaved by a being called the Ancient One.

The Ancient One was an aboleth that had hunted the waters of the world before the first gods arrived. The humans it had taken were no longer human: they had become aboleth spawn, living a blissful, ignorant life without pain or care. While the band talked with the creature, Oberon decided that such slavery could not be allowed to stand and attacked it. He nearly died, and in his dreams Solmir asked him to swear a new oath to win salvation: slay all the corrupted. Oberon returned to life bound to that oath and went on a killing rampage among the spawn. The band had to decide what to do with a man too unstable to be left loose, while around them the spawn regained their memories and, in the confusion, began slaughtering one another. The band left for Sporefall and left the creatures to their fate.

The Forging of Godfalla

Korraneth woke soon after. With the Warden's blade severing him from his divinity, the band fought and killed him, and Bram's dagger drank his essence. Then Nyssara revealed that one essence more was needed, and gave her own, leaving the future in Bram's hands. So the god-slaying weapon Godfalla was made.

Oberon was now judged too dangerous to keep. The band disarmed him, took his armour and left him restrained in the dark to fend for himself, and set out for Aurith Solmir.

Aurith Solmir

Bram broke the barrier, and the band stood before Solmir. Unlike the other gods, Solmir had made himself a being that acted only on his twenty-one oaths, his mind sealed away and preserved for the hour it would be needed. Velzaar appeared, restored that mind in the final hour, and left. Solmir revealed the truth of their world: it had been an experiment, to see whether humanity could overcome its nature and escape its destiny of destruction. The Path had been meant to force humanity to look inward instead of worshipping external beings and seeking power outside itself. Some of the band understood then that they had not walked the Path as it was intended, having struck bargains with devils and done whatever their loved ones required. If humanity was judged worthy, salvation waited with Velzaar. If not, it would be destroyed.

Then Oberon reached Solmir, and learned the truth of his visions. The Solmir who had spoken to him was Hythrea, who had used him to bring his essence close, through the knight's corrupted holy sword, and strike a surprise blow that left Solmir mortally wounded. With the last of his divine essence, and with the sacrifice of the hundred companions who still served him in death, Solmir swore a twenty-second oath and fought beside the band against Hythrea. Oberon abandoned his friends and sided with her. The battle was hard, but Solmir and the band prevailed, and with his last breath Solmir left the future in their hands. Oberon had crossed a line from which there was no return. Clay searched for a way to redeem him; Milo ended the question, and killed him.

The Judgement of Velzaar

The last trial was Velzaar's. He told the band that their character and the choices of their whole journey would now be weighed, and that if they were worthy, an Ark waited, carrying the last survivors of the world and most of the people of Revathra, whom the wizard-god had saved. Before judgement could be passed, the devil who had signed contracts with some of them appeared and argued that it was the better candidate to decide the Ark's fate and to receive the divine power needed to found a new world. It named every mistake, every consequence, every flaw and weakness of the band, to prove them unworthy. Velzaar found the argument insufficient, struck the devil down with a falling star, and judged the band worthy.

Bram, as the one who came closest to embodying the Path, was given god-like power to use wisely in the settling of a new world, and command of the Ark, which was already set for a new planet. Velzaar went out into the universe to explore other worlds and left the fate of humanity and the other peoples with the band. The Ark crossed the void for one hundred years, and came to the world that is now called Aurelis.

4. A Brief History of Aurelis

No founder, hero, nation, or victim is exempt from honest history.Charter of the University of the Five

The Death of the Old World

The peoples who founded Aurelis came from Cael'Vatra, itself a refuge built after an earlier cataclysm called Veltrazhan, the Shattering. The final destruction began with the death of Hythrea, god of the Elevaari. Her ruined essence became the Blight, and the surviving gods abandoned the plane rather than face what followed. The whole of that story is told in The Story So Far.

Humanity and the other peoples of the old world had spent generations treating one another as enemies. When extinction became unavoidable, a few chose differently. Several Durnin kings swore non-hostility and protection toward humans in exchange for a place aboard the Ark. The storm, cloud, and frost lineages of the Golgoth, disgusted by what the Blight had made of their ancestral lands, sided with humanity and helped complete the vessel. The peoples of Sporefall carried their subterranean traditions into the future. The spawn of the aboleths were remade into the people now called Proteans.

The Dawn of Apotheosis

At the end of the old world, a band of companions found Velzaar's Ark, and Bram Alderwind was granted power great enough to reshape the future. He used it not to rule, but to establish the conditions under which the survivors might build a society that would not repeat Cael'Vatra's failures.

The companions did not all live to see it. Oberon, once an Avowed knight, chose the Blight in the final hour and was destroyed. History remembers him as the Betrayer. The rest reached the Ark, and the vessel departed carrying the preserved peoples, their knowledge, and the living species of a world that would not survive them. How they came to the Ark, and what Velzaar asked of them there, is told in The Story So Far.

The Companions of the Dawn

Their names are kept in the history books. The stories kept their titles.

  • Bram the FounderBram Alderwind, who was given the power of a god and spent it building a world he then refused to rule.
  • Heckleberry the Unlikely HeroHeckleberry Finn, the Chronicler, a historian of Sporefall who joined the Companions after Kaelen fell. His shield, the Unfinished Chronicle, became the Finished Chronicle, and his school became the Five.
  • Milo the TricksterMilo Vexler, who founded the Haven of the Lost Stars so that no child would be without a name or a future.
  • Clay the GiantbornClay Rostov, who carried the sword Twilight and whose kin became the first Runeborn.
  • Theodore the UnluckyA monk who stood with the companions when the end came. At the height of his power he was destroyed by a stray spell in the battle against the Simulacrum of the lich Irenicus: the wrong place, the wrong moment.
  • Kaelen the MartyrBound by an old bargain with a devil. When the devil came for its due in the battle against Irenicus, Kaelen refused and was killed where he stood. His last words to his companions were "Make it count."
  • Oberon the BetrayerAn Avowed knight who sided with the Blight at the last. The Custodes, who descend from the Avowed, carry his memory as a wound and a warning.

The Hundred-Year Crossing

The Ark crossed the void for one hundred years while its passengers slept in stasis. This period is called the Long Night, the Crossing, or the Century of Sleep, depending on region and tradition. Nothing is remembered of it by those who slept, which has not prevented a great deal from being written about it.

Landfall and the Founding

The survivors awakened on Aurelis, seven hundred and forty-three years ago, and founded Zenith on the northern shore of Goldwater Bay. Their task was not only to build homes, but to decide what kind of civilization should replace the one they had lost. Bram used his remaining power to establish the principles of the new order:

  • the Path would stand at the centre of civic life;
  • Guest Right would apply to all recognized peoples;
  • a common coin would be accepted throughout the settled lands;
  • healing and education would be public duties rather than privileges;
  • no nation would be permitted to develop weapons capable of destroying peoples or regions;
  • a neutral order would intervene when conflicts threatened the peace of the whole world.

Three of these he did not leave to law alone. He bound free healing and education, the sanctity of Guest Right, and the prohibition of weapons of destruction into the world as the Three Edicts, enforced by a curse that no one has yet escaped. When this work was complete, Bram laid down his power and walked out of history. Where he went, and how he died, no record says.

The First Institutions

Heckleberry Finn founded the University of the Five, usually called simply the Five. Its purpose was not to glorify the past, but to preserve it accurately, including the failures, compromises, and crimes of revered figures. His shield, the Finished Chronicle, became its most sacred object.

Milo Vexler founded the Haven of the Lost Stars, an orphanage, school, and refuge for children without family or citizenship. Its graduates are known for entering law, diplomacy, medicine, and public service.

Bram raised Avalon on the island at the centre of Goldwater Bay and founded the Custodes there, in the spirit of the Avowed of the old world: a knightly order bound to no crown, charged with keeping the peace of all and the war of none.

The people of Sporefall, who had lived in the deep places of the old world, chose the open air of the new one. They settled the Jade Canopy and founded Sporerise among the roots and lower boughs of its greatest tree, a new society built on the old principles of mutual obligation. Their ancient Warden, Threxil Zai'Ryn, returned to the home he had sought for a thousand years, promising to come back from time to time.

Thormund, last of the Volmyr, the fire giants of the old world, taught the lore of runes to the kin of Clay the Giantborn. From this teaching came modern rune magic and, in time, a new people, the Runeborn. Thormund himself passed out of the histories somewhere in the first centuries. Whether he lives still in some high place, or died unrecorded, no one can say, and the rune schools quarrel about it to this day.

The Age of Rebuilding

The first generations lived in a technological dark age. They possessed preserved knowledge, but lacked mines, roads, workshops, farms, and the immense web of skilled labour needed to use it. Magic kept communities alive while institutions were rebuilt.

The father of Kaelen the Martyr created the first new automatons from his work with a shield guardian. These early constructs were not sentient. They served as labourers, guardians, and rescue workers, and their descendants still do.

The Age of Engines

Over centuries, rune-assisted steam power transformed Aurelis. Railways joined the central lands. Airships crossed the Wyrmspine and the seas. Automatons became common in mines, ports, construction, and hazardous work.

The settled world did not industrialize its weapons at the same rate. The Third Edict bans scalable engines of mass killing outright, and it is widely held that volatile propellants and destructive pressure systems are unusually vulnerable to the Law of Chaos. Civilian engines are stabilized by expensive runes and licensed engineers; weapons designed for rapid, repeatable killing are difficult to stabilize, illegal to make, and treated as a threat to the Concord itself. The Office of Measured Craft was founded to draw and police that line.

The Warden's Return and the Astral Trade

When Warden Threxil Zai'Ryn returned to his home plane, he found his people, the Zerth, in a brutal civil war. He intervened, brought news of what had happened on Cael'Vatra, and quelled the insurgent rebellion, restoring peace to the Astral Plane.

During that war, children born with four arms had been persecuted and purged as abominations. Threxil saved some of them and entrusted them to Bram. Raised mostly by humans in the ways of the Path, they became the people known as the Velkari.

The Warden then established trade between the Astral Plane and Aurelis. Warden's Reach, in the Blackreef Isles, is the principal trading post, with outposts at Saltgate on the Iron Sea and Stormbreak on the Shifting Sea. The Zerth, like everyone else on Aurelis, are bound by the Edicts while they stand on its soil.

The Long Concord

For generations, no war grew large enough to engulf the settled world. Trade expanded. The Path became ordinary civic practice rather than a desperate response to apocalypse. The Founders became history, then legend, then symbols used by political movements that often disagreed about what the Founders had actually meant.

The Present Day

It is the seven hundred and forty-third year since Landfall, the eight hundred and forty-third since the Dawn. Aurelis is prosperous beyond anything the old world knew. It is also a world in which the old certainties have begun, quietly, to be questioned.

Rumours on the Road

What follows is not history. It is what you will hear in wayhouses, and you should weigh it accordingly.

  • The Starfall Society claims to have found the first credible clues to the fate of the Ark, and has sent expeditions to the floating islands and into the Great Depths to chase them.
  • In the Blackreef Isles, a Protean movement calling itself the First Memory says its people are dreaming memories that were taken from them before Landfall, and demands the archives of the Five be opened.
  • Inventors in Brightwater and Vaalgard complain that the Office of Measured Craft has become a brake on all invention, not only the forbidden kind. The Office replies that this is exactly what the Founding asked of it.
  • An automaton in a Verdant Reach mill is said to have spoken a sentence no one taught it. The Office calls it a malfunction. The millworkers call it by name.

5. The Path

The Laws are not tools. They are mirrors. They reveal who you are.The Scroll of Becoming

The Path is the most influential philosophy in Aurelis. It is not formally a religion, and it does not require worship of the Five, of Bram, or of any god. It is a civic and personal discipline built from the lessons of Cael'Vatra, and it rests on three gifts: a truth, a creed, and a way.

  1. The Five Laws of the Universe: the metaphysical truths that shape reality and the self.
  2. The Apethos Creed: five moral starting points drawn from the virtues and failures of the Five.
  3. The Scroll of Becoming: a living body of practical teaching on discipline, growth, community, and self-mastery.

The Five Laws

Each Law is a force that shapes both the cosmos and the inner self. Everyone is believed to hold a spark of each, but most resonate more strongly with one.

The first four Laws were known in Cael'Vatra. Bram the Founder named Chaos as the fifth. Older temples, manuscripts, and traditionalists still use fourfold imagery, which has produced centuries of debate over whether Chaos is a Law, a condition between Laws, or the freedom that makes the other Laws meaningful.

The Apethos Creed

The Creed takes its name from Apotheosis, the rising beyond mortality by virtue. Its five tenets are carved into the halls of the Five and memorized by every child.

VoiceTenet
Velzaar"Shape with vision, not conquest." Seek knowledge not for power, but for creation.
Solmir"Let your fire illuminate, not consume." Purpose must inspire, not dominate.
Korraneth"Stand. Even when there is no reason to." Endurance is a virtue born from choice, not reward.
Nyssara"Express truth, even if it wears a mask." Honesty can be art; passion, a kind of prayer.
Elaria"Flow with what is, so you may shape what will be." Harmony is not passivity. It is power made whole.

The Five are not presented as flawless saints. The University insists that the Creed has value only when it is taught beside the consequences of their choices. The tenets are not rules. They are starting points for Becoming.

The Scroll of Becoming

The Scroll is not one fixed book. It is passed from teacher to student, copied by hand, commented upon, and adapted for the self, and it is taught differently across Aurelis. Its themes are constant: life is not static, and you are meant to change; all struggle is fuel for growth; growth without virtue is corruption in disguise; master the self, not the world; serve the community without losing the soul.

Its most widely taught chapters are:

  • On Silence and Listening: the foundation, on discipline and inner stillness. "Only when the mind is still, may the self be heard."
  • The Four Faces of Flame: the Laws as fire: the gentle hearth, the sacred torch, the wild blaze, the slow ember.
  • The Crucible Within: the outer trial as a reflection of the inner one.
  • Of Masks and Mirrors: a meditation on identity, honesty, and the role of self-expression in truth.
  • The Garden and the Forge: parables contrasting preservation and transformation, teaching that both are sacred.
  • On Walking the Edge: a reflection for those nearing death, completing a great trial, or passing through transformation.
  • The Fifth Turning: a later chapter on Chaos, invention, and the danger of confusing disruption with growth. Not every school teaches it, and some teach against it.

The simplest expression of the Path remains the litany every child learns:

To change, even when it hurts.
To create, even when all is ash.
To endure, even when the world does not.
To listen, even when there is only silence.
To belong, even when walking alone.

No one finishes the Path. But those who do not walk it are already lost.

6. Law, Government and Everyday Society

I come as one voice among many, not as the voice of my people alone.The Oath of the Many

The Concord of Aurelis

Aurelis is not one nation. It is a collection of kingdoms, republics, city-states, enclaves, and ancestral territories bound by a Founding compact known as the Concord.

Members of the Concord keep their own laws, rulers, and traditions, but accept five shared obligations:

  1. honour Guest Right;
  2. accept the common coin;
  3. provide healing and education to all;
  4. submit disputes that threaten wider war to neutral arbitration;
  5. prohibit the development and deployment of catastrophic weapons.

Three of these are also Edicts, enforced by Bram's curse whether or not a ruler agrees to them. The other two are kept by treaty and custom, with the Custodes standing between any two states that forget them.

Zenith and the Council of Many Voices

Zenith is the capital of the Concord and the symbolic centre of the Path. It is governed, and the Concord is convened, by a representative council. Each delegate swears the Oath of the Many:

I come as one voice among many, not as the voice of my people alone.
I will speak what is true, though it shames my kin.
I will name my interests before I vote on them.
I will not raise my hand against another delegation in this hall or beyond it.
I will abide the Council's judgement when it goes against me, and seek its reversal only by lawful word. When my term ends I will lay down this seat, and go.
I swear it by the ones who came before me, and before those gathered, who will not forget.
If I break this vow, let my voice fail in this hall and let me live with the consequence in shame.

The oath is not ceremonial. It is a paladin's oath, and the council chamber is built to recognize it. A delegate who knowingly breaks it may literally lose the ability to speak within the hall. More severe breaches can damage the oath-taker's sense of identity and wholeness. This council oath is a different thing from the Mark of the Edicts, though both are inescapable.

Bram's Three Edicts

Before giving away his power, Bram the Founder used it to bind three absolute Edicts into Aurelis itself.

  1. Education and healing must be free for everyone. Neither is a privilege reserved for those who can afford it.
  2. Guest Right is protected. No violence is permitted when a guest has been invited into a home, or is seeking refuge from the road.
  3. Weapons of mass destruction, and any advancement of craft aimed at creating weapons, are forbidden. Swords and armour, yes. Firearms and bombs, never.

These are the Three Edicts in full. The common coin, the Council's oath, and the duties of the Custodes are related features of the Founding, not additional Edicts.

The Mark and the Curse

A person who breaks an Edict is cursed. A visible mark appears on their forehead, branding them as an outlaw before any court has spoken. The curse's shape varies; its severity and its certainty do not.

Sometimes it takes the form of a sickness that kills the bearer within two or three days. Sometimes it cripples. The consequence is always extreme, and it is always guaranteed. In seven centuries no exemption, cure, or evasion has ever been reliably recorded, and those who claim to sell one are frauds.

The Edicts are therefore more than criminal laws enforced when witnesses or officials happen to be present. They are part of the foundation of the world, and the world keeps them itself.

Revenants

Very rarely, a person the curse has killed does not stay dead. Days or years after burial they rise and walk again, the mark still on their brow. The University calls them Revenants, and admits plainly that it does not understand them.

Three explanations are argued. Some hold that a Revenant is returned for a purpose, and that the Founding has further use for them. Others hold that they are a flaw in the design of the Edicts, a seam in the weave where the curse's work was left unfinished. A third school holds that the return is the curse itself, continued: that for certain breaches death was never meant to be the end of the sentence, and a Revenant is a punishment without term.

Their number is small because so few people break the Edicts at all. A handful are believed to date from the first years after Landfall, when the Edicts were newly bound and the shape of the curse was not yet understood, and these are the oldest living people on Aurelis. Whatever brought them back, nothing has yet been seen to kill them again. Revenants do not age, and they do not die.

Guest Right

Guest Right is one of the most beloved and most argued-over institutions in Aurelis. The Edict protects two situations absolutely: a guest invited across a threshold, and a traveller seeking refuge from the road. Within that protection no hand may be raised, by host or guest.

Custom has built a practical system around the Edict. Any peaceful traveller may ask clean water, a basic meal, medical stabilization, and one night's shelter from a registered wayhouse, temple, civic hall, or participating household. The guest must declare dangerous weapons, obey the host's rules, and contribute labour or coin when able. Local treasuries compensate poor hosts.

Refusing Guest Right without cause is a serious civic shame. Abusing it is a crime. Striking under it is death.

Education and Healing

Every recognized settlement is expected to maintain access to schooling and healing. Larger cities operate public academies, clinics, and Houses of Mending. Remote communities rely on travelling teachers, druids, oath-healers, and automaton-supported medical caravans.

The right is universal in law, but not equal in practice. Zenith and Silvermere offer care that frontier communities cannot match. This gap is one of the most persistent criticisms of the Concord, and the Edict does not close it on its own. It only guarantees that no one may be charged for what they receive.

Oaths and Power

Paladin oaths are among the strongest sources of personal power in Aurelis because they bind the whole self. Breaking an oath is not merely losing access to magic. Oathbreakers describe it as losing a part of themselves that cannot be replaced.

Some attempt redemption. Few succeed. Many seek dangerous service, sacrifice, or death rather than live with the absence.

Because of this, Aurelis treats the recruitment of paladins as a serious legal and ethical matter. Oaths sworn under coercion are disputed, and institutions that pressure children or debtors into sacred vows are widely condemned.

7. Magic and the Ways Power Is Gained

A rune is not a mark placed on a thing. It is a proposition about what that thing is allowed to become.Runeborn teaching, Red Mesa

Magic in Aurelis reflects relationship, commitment, study, and identity. The Laws describe the forces of the world; the traditions below describe the ways people come to wield them.

Arcane Practice

Arcane power is gained through study, experimentation, craft, and disciplined understanding. The University of the Five, the Durnin rune schools, the Elevaari colleges of Ionia and Mistwood, and independent guilds all teach different traditions. Many Elevaari turned to arcane study after rejecting the worship of gods; the Myrin are drawn to it by temperament; the Velkari show a particular affinity for it.

Paladin Oaths

A paladin gains power by binding the self to an oath with genuine cost. The more restrictive and sincerely held the oath, the greater its potential power, and the greater the damage caused by breaking it. The Oath of the Many and the vows of the Custodes both belong to this tradition.

Clerical Power

A god may grant power to a worshipper, but gods deal cautiously with Aurelis. The peoples of this world helped kill a god and survived the consequences. Open divine intervention is rare, politically charged, and often treated as a possible invasion rather than a blessing.

Druidic Devotion

Druidic power comes from devoted service to the balance of an element, an ecosystem, a species, or a place. A druid is not merely a mage of nature; they are a custodian bound to something living and specific.

Pacts and Contracts

Warlock power is gained through bargains. Aurelis has extensive laws governing magical contracts, but no legal code can make a desperate person read every clause or recognize every hidden cost. Every child knows how Kaelen the Martyr died.

Rune Magic

Rune magic descends from Thormund's teaching. It combines language, pattern, memory, material, and intent. Among the Runeborn it is both a living craft and part of a people's origin: practising it over generations has attuned them to the elements and changed their very bodies.

Psionics

The Zerth of the Astral Plane are psionic beings who, on their home plane, can shape matter by thought alone. On Aurelis their gifts are quieter, but real. The Velkari, raised among humans, carry the same inheritance, often expressed through the Arcane rather than the mind alone.

Chaos Magic

Chaos magic is the youngest formally recognized discipline. It rewards invention and improvisation but resists standardization. Its practitioners are celebrated as innovators and distrusted as hazards, often at the same time, and the Office of Measured Craft watches them closely.

8. Technology

Build what carries. Never build what only kills.Inscription over the Office of Measured Craft, Brightwater

Aurelis lives in a rune-steam age. The things an ordinary person sees in a lifetime include:

  • steam-powered, rune-stabilized trains along the central lines;
  • airships used for trade, diplomacy, exploration, and the transport of the Custodes;
  • dock cranes, pumps, lifts, and agricultural engines;
  • non-sentient automatons used as workers, guards, and rescue constructs;
  • magical communication relays in the major cities;
  • advanced healing supported by alchemy, divine practice, and public infrastructure.

Weapons and armour remain closer to the old world's than to its engines. The Third Edict does not only forbid weapons large enough to destroy a city. It forbids any advancement of craft aimed at creating weapons: no firearms, no bombs, no engine whose purpose is to let an untrained hand kill quickly and at scale. A weapon's intended benefit does not exempt its maker from the Mark.

It is the certainty of the Edict, rather than the limits of engineering or the vigilance of constables, that keeps the boundary. Even so, forbidden arms research exists. The very laws that kept Aurelis peaceful have made outlawed weapon designs among the most valuable secrets in the world, and hunting them is the quietest and most serious work of the Office of Measured Craft.

Part II

People and Places

9. The Peoples of Aurelis

No people survived alone.The second lesson

Aurelis was founded by peoples who had once considered one another enemies. Their coexistence is real, but it is not effortless, and it is not finished.

Humans

Found everywhere; most numerous in Zenith, Everdawn Vale, the Verdant Reach and the Sunscar Expanse.

Humans remain the most numerous and widespread people of Aurelis. Since Landfall they have continued to change, deepening their attunement to the Laws that shape reality, and human cultures tend to interpret the Laws through vocation. Arcane lineages dominate scholarship and engineering; Divine lineages produce judges, oathkeepers, and civic servants; Primal lineages are common among soldiers, athletes, and frontier communities; Elemental traditions flourish among sailors, farmers, and wardens; Chaos-attuned communities are associated with invention, performance, and reform.

These are tendencies, not castes. Humans also raised most of the Velkari children entrusted to Bram, and the two peoples remain close.

Elevaari

Seat Ionia, in the Hylian Wilds. Also Mistwood, Greyfen, the Jade Canopy.

The Elevaari carry the memory of Hythrea, their dead god, and of the catastrophe that followed his death. The survivors who boarded the Ark resolved that no god should ever again hold such power over their people, and most Elevaari traditions reject worship outright.

Some turned toward the forests and the long stewardship of living things. Others embraced arcane study, arguing that knowledge is safer when it can be questioned, taught, and revised. Their relationship with the Path is complicated: many respect its discipline but resent human attempts to treat Cael'Vatra's end as a shared tragedy when the death of Hythrea remains, to them, a specifically Elevaari wound.

Durnin

Seat Vaalgard, beneath Ashkar's Pyre, shared with the Golgoth. Also the holds of the Wyrmspine.

The Durnin survived because several ancient kings swore a pact with Velzaar: non-hostility and protection toward humanity in exchange for a place in the new world.

Whether that oath still binds their descendants is one of the great constitutional disputes of Aurelis. Traditionalists treat it as a sacred debt. Reformers argue that no people can inherit permanent obligation from ancestors they never chose. The dispute has never come to blows, which the Durnin themselves regard as the pact working as intended.

Durnin communities are central to mining, rail engineering, rune craft, and heavy construction. The forges of Vaalgard and the tunnels of the Wyrmspine are their work.

Golgoth

Seat Vaalgard, shared with the Durnin. Lineages Skaldrin in Skalgard and the Frostveil Crown; Raukor at Stormbreak and the western seas; Vaelori in the high citadels of the Wyrmspine.

Golgoth is the broad name for the giant peoples who escaped the old world. Three lineages survived: the Skaldrin, frost giants of the north; the Raukor, storm giants who rule the western ports; and the Vaelori, cloud giants of the high citadels and the air routes. All three sided with humanity when the Blight consumed their ancient lands, helped build the Ark, and came to Aurelis seeking coexistence rather than dominion.

A fourth lineage, the Volmyr, the fire giants, did not survive as a people. Thormund, the last of them, taught the runes and then passed out of history. The Runeborn are his legacy, but they are not Volmyr, and no one can say with certainty whether the last Volmyr still breathes.

Myrin

Origin Sporefall. Found in every city with a library.

The Myrin are a short people descended from the ingenious folk of old Sporefall. Adventurous, resourceful, and incurably curious, they are drawn to arcane learning and invention, and many become wizards, teachers, engineers, and accomplished librarians. Although their roots are in Sporefall, the Myrin long ago spread to every corner of Aurelis rather than remaining in Sporerise.

Tavren

Origin Sporefall. Found the Verdant Reach, the Oasis of Quesh, Zenith, Sporerise.

The Tavren are a short people known for courage out of proportion to their size. Tricksters, farmers, and family-centred folk are common among them. Their ancestors came from Sporefall, and today they are found in the canals and farms of the Verdant Reach, around the Oasis of Quesh, in the districts of Zenith, and still, in numbers, in Sporerise.

Nhalari

Seat Sporerise, in the Jade Canopy.

The Nhalari are the dark-adapted people of Sporefall, and the people who most fully kept its ways. They are the heart of Sporerise, the forest city founded after Landfall on the old principles of mutual obligation, practical knowledge, and the memory of surviving without the protection of a god or a nation. Their ancestors lived without the sun; the Nhalari live under the Canopy, which filters it to their liking. Of all the peoples of Aurelis, they are the ones the rest of the Concord knows least, and they prefer it that way.

Proteans

Seat Anamnis, in the Blackreef Isles. Also Saltbloom Isle, Pearlwind Cay, the southern coasts.

The Proteans descend from the spawn of the aboleths, remade during the escape from the old world. Their ancestors were given humanoid forms, the ability to breathe air, human-like lifespans, and the capacity to reproduce without a partner. Their memories from before the death of the Ancient One were erased.

Protean flesh remains unusually adaptable. Over time, individuals can alter themselves in response to environment, injury, vocation, or deliberate practice. Some changes are subtle: lungs suited to thin air, eyes suited to darkness, skin hardened against salt. Others are dramatic. Protean identity is therefore less tied to fixed appearance than in most cultures of Aurelis, and kinship is often defined through chosen line, shared memory, and the person from whom one budded.

Runeborn

Seat the rune-forges of the Red Mesa. Also Vaalgard, the Wyrmspine.

The Runeborn descend from the kin of Clay the Giantborn and carry giant blood. They developed rune magic alongside Thormund the Volmyr, becoming attuned to the runes and to the elements, and over the generations that practice changed them: they grew larger, and the Arcane and the Primal run together in them.

Their identity is bound to ancestry, to a shared craft, and to the question of whether the teacher who shaped them still lives. Every rune school claims a piece of Thormund's legacy; the Runeborn claim all of it.

Zerth

Home the Astral Plane. On Aurelis Warden's Reach, Saltgate, Stormbreak.

The Zerth are the people of Warden Threxil Zai'Ryn: psionic beings who, on their home plane, can shape matter with thought alone. The Warden returned to find them in a brutal civil war, intervened, and restored peace to the Astral Plane. He then opened trade with Aurelis.

Zerth merchants, factors, and envoys are a familiar sight at the three trading posts and an exotic one anywhere else. They are bound by the Edicts while on Aurelis, like everyone else, and their origin grants them no licence to build what the Third Edict forbids.

Velkari

Origin the Astral Plane. Raised among humans; found wherever humans are.

During the Zerth civil war, children were born with four arms. Their people called them impure and purged them. Warden Threxil saved some of the children and asked Bram the Founder to care for them. Raised mostly by humans and taught the Path, they became the Velkari, whose name in the Zerth tongue means "the abandoned."

The Velkari carry the psionic inheritance of their ancestors and a marked affinity for the Arcane. They are few, and they are not forgotten: among humans, a Velkari at the table is a reminder of what the Founding was for.

Dracaryn

Origin the dragon Aurelith. Found scattered; many serve among the Custodes.

The Dracaryn are the mortal descendants of the dragon Aurelith. Their lineage began when she allowed her child to mingle with humans so that the line might continue. Their lifespans extend beyond eight hundred years.

They are curious, pleasure-loving people, fond of food and of the experiences a long life offers, and they regard fighting as a discipline rather than as a violence to be enjoyed. That temperament, and their patience, suits the Custodes, and a Dracaryn knight of Avalon is a common figure in the stories.

10. Institutions and Factions

Bound to no crown, we answer to all.Motto of the Custodes

The Custodes

Seat Avalon, the tower-island in Goldwater Bay. Chapter houses Highwatch on the Wyrmspine, and in most major cities. Founded by Bram the Founder, in the spirit of the Avowed.

The Custodes swear service to no crown, city, ancestry, or faction. They are the Concord's neutral constables, its peacekeepers, and its monster hunters. They police the roads and the frontier where no local law reaches, stand between states that are drifting toward war, and ride against the beasts no town can face alone. The Edicts need no knights to enforce them; the Custodes exist for everything the Edicts do not cover.

When the first leviathan rose, long after the Founding, it was the Custodes who answered, and they have answered every sighting since, though there is no word of it in their vow. The vow is older than the leviathans.

Their vow is short, and every child can recite it:

I hold no banner. I keep the peace of all and the war of none.
Where the law has no hand and the road has no watch, I am the hand and the watch.
What hunts the people, I will hunt.
I will not be bought, and I will not be first to draw.

The Custodes descend from the Avowed of the old world, and they carry that inheritance with pride and with one old shame: Oberon the Betrayer was an Avowed before he was anything else. Every Custodes novice is taught his story on the first day, so that none of them mistakes neutrality for virtue. The order is strongest when it is widely trusted. It is most dangerous when it begins to believe that neutrality makes it infallible.

The University of the Five

Seat Zenith. Founded by Heckleberry the Unlikely Hero. Symbol a five-part open circle surrounding an unfinished line.

The Five was founded to preserve history and teach the moral consequences of the past. It trains historians, judges, engineers, diplomats, physicians, cartographers, and teachers of the Path. Its most important artefact is the Finished Chronicle, the founder's shield, which records the fall of Cael'Vatra, Revathra, the final journeys of the Companions, and their meeting with Velzaar.

The University's central doctrine is uncomfortable by design: no founder, hero, nation, or victim is exempt from honest history.

The Haven of the Lost Stars

Seat Zenith. Founded by Milo the Trickster. Public role orphanage, school, refuge, civil-service academy.

The Haven accepts children who have lost family, citizenship, or place, and gives them education, legal identity, and a path into adult life. Its graduates are unusually prominent in diplomacy, law, and information work.

Rumour holds that some of them belong to a hidden adult order, called the Quiet Constellation, of oath-bound investigators who expose corruption before it becomes war. The Haven neither confirms nor denies this, which most people take as confirmation.

The Council of Many Voices

Seat Zenith. Role government of the capital; diplomacy, arbitration and law of the Concord; recognition of new states.

Delegates are representatives of their peoples, but their oath requires them to act as one voice among many. The Council is respected, resented, and frequently accused of allowing Zenith to disguise influence as neutrality. The Custodes are not its army, and the Council is not their master; the two institutions have spent seven centuries reminding each other of this.

The Office of Measured Craft

Seats Zenith, Vaalgard and Brightwater. Role licenses dangerous engines, inspects rune systems, suppresses forbidden weapons research.

The Office is a joint institution of the Five and the Council. It decides what an engine is for, which is to say it decides what is allowed to be built. Inventors regard it as either the reason Aurelis survived or the reason progress has stalled, and the Office regards both opinions as evidence that it is doing its job.

The Starfall Society

Seat Zenith, in the halls of the Five. Role expeditions, cipher scholarship, and the search for the Founding's lost places.

The Society has no city of its own. It has the University's patience, the Council's grudging funding, and an inexhaustible supply of scholars who believe the Ark was not destroyed. Its expeditions go wherever a clue points: the floating islands, the Great Depths, the ruins of Founding-era works, the deep forges of the Red Mesa. The Society insists it seeks history, not technology. Few believe that distinction will survive the discovery of anything useful.

The First Memory

Base the Blackreef Isles, strongest in Anamnis and on Pearlwind Cay. Role Protean ancestral-rights movement.

Some Proteans report inherited memories that should not exist: places, faces, and a death they never lived. The First Memory holds that the Proteans were remade and had their past erased without consent, and that the Founding owes them the truth. Its moderates demand access to the archives of the Five and control over all study of Protean flesh. It is said to have a harder wing that wants more than archives, but no one who says so will give a name.

The Zerth Trading Posts

Principal post Warden's Reach. Outposts Saltgate, Stormbreak.

The trade Warden Threxil founded links Aurelis with the Astral Plane. The three posts are extraterritorial in custom but not in law: the Edicts apply within their walls as everywhere else. Supporters see new knowledge and allies. Opponents fear dependency, espionage, and the arrival of powers unconstrained by the Concord. Both sides agree the posts are the most interesting places on Aurelis to be a merchant.

11. Regions of the Known World

A map is a promise the land has not agreed to keep.Saying of the cartographers of the Five

What follows reads the map from the centre outward. Hold it beside you as you read.

Zenith and Goldwater Bay

Settlements Zenith, Solis, Brightwater, Avalon.

Zenith stands at the political and symbolic centre of Aurelis, on the northern shore of the great bay where the rivers of the Wyrmspine and Everdawn come down to the sea. The University of the Five, the Haven of the Lost Stars, the Council of Many Voices, the principal archives, and the largest Houses of Mending are all here, and the rail lines of the continent meet beneath its walls.

Goldwater Bay is the busiest water in the world. Solis, on its western shore under the mountains, is the bay's oldest fishing town and its quietest. Brightwater, on the eastern shore, builds the airships, licenses the engines, and houses a seat of the Office of Measured Craft.

Avalon

Seat of the Custodes.

At the heart of the bay rises the tower-island of Avalon, built by Bram the Founder and given to the Custodes. The great tower is visible from Zenith on a clear day, and its beacon is lit when the order rides. Avalon is a fortress, a school of arms, a court of arbitration, and a hall of records; it is not a city, and the Custodes are careful that it should never become one.

Frostveil Crown

Settlements Skalgard, White Harbor. Waters the Frigid Verge.

A vast northern region of glaciers, high valleys, and old giant settlements. Skalgard is the city of the Skaldrin, the frost-lineage Golgoth, and the strongest voice of the north. White Harbor, on the northern coast, connects the Crown to the central rail network and the sea lanes. The region values autonomy, endurance, and ancestral law, and its delegates are the Council's most reliable sceptics.

The Wyrmspine

Settlements Highwatch; the Durnin holds; the Vaelori citadels.

The mountain chain that divides the north and west from central Aurelis. Durnin holds, cloud-giant citadels, and rail tunnels all compete for space in it. Highwatch, on the southern slopes above the river road to Zenith, is the Custodes' great chapter house of the interior; from it the order can reach any part of the central lands by rail or airship within days. The Wyrmspine is both the physical backbone of the continent and its most important overland corridor.

Hylian Wilds

Settlements Ionia, Mistwood, Greyfen, Hollowmere. Waters the Shifting Sea.

The great ancient forest of the west, home to the Elevaari, to free settlements, and to old ecological pacts that predate the Concord. Ionia, deep in the forest, is the seat of the Elevaari and the place where their long memory is kept. Mistwood, to its north, is a centre of forest scholarship and ecological archive. Greyfen joins the forest to the western coast and its marshes, and Hollowmere, among the lakes at the forest's southern edge, is the last settled place before the desert.

Everdawn Vale

Settlements Silvermere, Dawnrest.

The richest agricultural and population centre outside Zenith, a green country of rivers running down to the bay. Silvermere and Dawnrest are major political and cultural powers; Dawnrest in particular is a centre of Path orthodoxy. The Vale's leaders often argue that prosperity creates an obligation to enforce the Concord more strongly across the world, an argument the north does not share.

Ashkar's Pyre

Settlements Vaalgard, Saltgate. Waters the Iron Sea.

The great volcano of the north-east and the scorched, mineral-rich country at its feet. Vaalgard, the fortress-city the Durnin and the Golgoth built together, climbs the lower slopes; its forges are fed by the mountain, and it is the second seat of the Office of Measured Craft. On the coast to the south-east, Saltgate is the Zerth outpost of the Iron Sea, and the strangest market on the eastern shore.

Red Mesa

Peoples the Runeborn.

A high plateau of red stone between the Sunscar Expanse and the central lowlands, cut by canyons and crowned with wind-carved towers. The Runeborn keep their rune-forges here, in the heat and the silence, and it is here that the rune schools believe Thormund taught. The Mesa has no city; it has holds, schools, and shrines, and it is not a place one enters uninvited.

Sunscar Expanse

Settlements Dunewatch, Calidan, the Oasis of Quesh.

A desert of old migration roads, oasis settlements, buried engines, and mobile communities. Dunewatch, on the southern coast, is its largest city and the terminus of the desert rail. Calidan, to its east, guards the approaches to the Mesa. The Oasis of Quesh, in the heart of the sands, is a human and Tavren settlement, a pilgrimage site, and the only reliable water for a hundred miles.

Verdant Reach

Settlements Calidan Pass; the canal towns.

A lowland of farms, canals, river towns, and old engineering works that feeds much of central Aurelis. The Tavren are its most numerous people. Calidan Pass, the fortified water-gate where the Reach meets the southern sea, controls the passage between the desert coast and the bay, and its tolls are a permanent subject of Council debate. The people of the Reach often feel that Zenith treats them as infrastructure rather than as a culture.

Jade Canopy

Settlements Sporerise, Nyssara's Veil.

A humid southern forest of enormous trees, waterfalls, river deltas, and deep root caverns. Sporerise, built among the roots and lower boughs of the greatest of the trees, is the city of the Nhalari and of the Tavren who stayed, and the Concord's chief authority on the caverns that open beneath the forest floor. The Nhalari decide who passes through it, in either direction. Nyssara's Veil, on the eastern coast, where the rivers fall into the sea, is a place of masks, festivals, and the old Elevaari rite of truth-telling from which it takes its name.

Blackreef Isles

Settlements Anamnis, Warden's Reach, Pearlwind Cay, Saltbloom Isle. Waters the Azure Current.

The southern archipelago, home to the Proteans, to independent ports, and to the greatest concentration of sea-adapted bodies in the known world. Anamnis, on the eastern isles, is the Protean seat and the heart of the First Memory. Warden's Reach, the ziggurat-crowned island in the west, is the principal Zerth trading post and the busiest foreign market on Aurelis. Saltbloom Isle is known for Protean healing craft and adaptive agriculture; Pearlwind Cay is a Protean lineage site, and the place where the first of the inherited memories were spoken aloud.

The Four Seas

Waters the Frigid Verge, the Shifting Sea, the Iron Sea, the Azure Current. Port Stormbreak.

The Frigid Verge lies north and west of the Crown, a sea of ice and whaling fleets. The Shifting Sea washes the western coast; Stormbreak, the Raukor port on its northern shore, is the western naval power and the third Zerth outpost. The Iron Sea breaks against the eastern coast below Ashkar's Pyre. The Azure Current runs south of the Blackreef Isles, warm, fast, and full of things that the Custodes keep a list of. Sailors on all four will tell you the same thing: the sea serpents on the maps are not decoration.

12. The Living Frontier

The peace is ours. The world is not.Saying of the Custodes

A Hostile World

Aurelis remains an incompletely explored and unconquered frontier. Dangerous creatures, bloodthirsty territorial beings, and powerful elementals inhabit its varied lands. The long peace describes relations among the settled peoples, not the temper of the wilderness. Leave the rail lines and the wayhouses, and civilization's reach ends quickly.

Leviathans

Leviathans are the most dangerous creatures on Aurelis: beings as large as cities, found in the seas, the deserts, and the mountains, and regarded as living calamities rather than animals. The Custodes are the first to answer a sighting. Most cities maintain shelters and drilled protocols, and a leviathan's passage is reckoned in the histories the way the old world reckoned wars.

The Unreconciled

Not every thinking creature on Aurelis has joined the Path or the Concord. Goblins and other such peoples of the deep wilds possess speech and cunning but regard all outsiders as invaders, and seven centuries of attempts have not produced a treaty that held. They are part of the frontier's danger, and a reminder that the peace was chosen, not given.

The Great Depths

Beneath Aurelis stretch vast caverns of strange flora and fauna. Much of this realm remains unexplored. The people of Sporerise fund expeditions to uncover its secrets, and the Starfall Society funds others; neither has yet found the bottom.

The Floating Islands

Islands move across the skies of Aurelis, carrying their own creatures and plants, some of them deadly and some of them medicinal. Expeditions sail or fly to them whenever one passes within reach. How they stay aloft, where they go, and what they were before they rose are questions no one has answered, and the Starfall Society has staked its reputation on the belief that the answers matter.

13. Relics of the Founding

Twilight

The sword of Clay the Giantborn, said to hold a fragment of the soul of Virelle Daos. It passed through his family as an heirloom, but no one has been able to attune to it since his death. The Runeborn keep it, and the rune schools have theories.

The Finished Chronicle

The shield of Heckleberry the Unlikely Hero, called the Unfinished Chronicle while he carried it, holds a living record of the Companions' journey, beginning with the fall of Cael'Vatra and Revathra and continuing through the final battles and the meeting with Velzaar. It is kept at the University of the Five in Zenith, and it is the one object on Aurelis that every historian agrees is telling the truth.

Godfalla

The dagger of Bram the Founder still exists somewhere in Aurelis. Its location is unknown. It is both a relic and a political weapon: anyone who finds it can claim a connection to the Founder, whether true or false, and the Council has already ruled, pre-emptively, that the claim would mean nothing.

The Ark

Public history teaches that Velzaar's Ark carried the Founders to Aurelis. Its fate after Landfall is unknown. Most scholars assume it was dismantled, destroyed, or hidden because its workings would have destabilized the new world. The Starfall Society assumes otherwise, and is looking.

So ends the primer. The rest you will learn on the road, as everyone does.
